Clearing submerged weeds from a Canberra irrigation dam with Clipper

Gilba Solutions · 21 November 2026 · Rewritten from Gilba's December 2024 case study

Site
Federal Golf Club, Canberra
Contact
Pete Dunn, Director of Agronomy
Problem
Aquatic weeds and algae in the storage dam
Product
Clipper aquatic herbicide (flumioxazin, Group 14)
Treated
First week of October 2024
Result
No visible weeds after three weeks

Why the dam mattered

Federal Golf Club is one of Canberra's best-known courses and sits among Australia's top 100. It has 18 championship bentgrass greens and limited water storage, and Canberra's summers are long, hot and dry. A clean irrigation supply is not optional.

In August 2024, the club's Director of Agronomy, Pete Dunn, reported weeds and algae building up in the storage dam beside the maintenance facility. A mild spell had brought the problem on earlier than usual.

What was growing

Gilba Solutions inspected the dam and found four problems at once: floating filamentous algae, milfoil, salvinia and leafy elodea. Several of these grow below the surface. That ruled out Agri-Tec, which only treats algae. Early-morning readings put the water at pH 7.6.

Choosing the product

Gilba compared several aquatic herbicides, looking at what each controls and how long the water would be off-limits afterwards. Clipper was chosen because so many of the weeds were submerged. Its active ingredient is flumioxazin, a fast-acting, selective contact herbicide in Group 14. Its 4-day withholding period was workable for bentgrass irrigation, and it comes with reduced protective equipment requirements.

Clipper comes as large effervescent tablets in water-soluble wrapping. They can be thrown straight into the water, or dissolved and sprayed from a boat. Jerry Spencer of Gilba is accredited to use it.

Besides the weeds found at Federal, Gilba lists Clipper as controlling cabomba, watermeal, Eurasian watermilfoil, water lettuce, duckweed and giant salvinia, among others. Check the label for the weeds and situations it is registered for.

The treatment

  • The dam held about 8 megalitres and was close to full.
  • At roughly 27 tablets per megalitre, the job needed 216 tablets.
  • The tablets were spread evenly across the whole surface.
  • Treatment was held until the first week of October 2024, because water temperature affects how well the herbicide works and early spring suited it best.

The result

Three weeks after treatment there were no aquatic weeds left on or below the surface, and the filamentous algae had disappeared too.
