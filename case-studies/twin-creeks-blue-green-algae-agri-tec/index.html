Bringing a blue-green algae bloom under control at Twin Creeks

Gilba Solutions · 21 November 2026 · Rewritten from Gilba's April 2022 case study

Site
Twin Creeks Golf and Country Club, Nepean Valley
Contact
Darren Turner, Course Manager
Problem
Suspected blue-green algae bloom in a dam
Product
Agri-Tec (226 g/L copper sulphate pentahydrate)
Constraint
Environmentally sensitive site
Result
Complete control within one week

The call

Twin Creeks hosted the NSW Open from 2017 to 2019 and was ranked 93rd in Australia by Australian Golf Digest in 2021. That year, Course Manager Darren Turner contacted Gilba Solutions about growth on the surface of one of the course dams. He suspected a blue-green algae bloom.

Confirming the cause

A site visit pointed strongly to blue-green algae (cyanobacteria), and water testing confirmed it. Blooms like this are usually driven by a combination of nutrient run-off (nitrogen and phosphorus), high temperatures and strong sunlight, and still or slow-moving water.

Working within the site's limits

The course is environmentally sensitive, so the treatment had to have as little impact as possible beyond the algae. Gilba recommended Agri-Tec, a proprietary copper formulation containing 226 g/L of copper sulphate pentahydrate. The reasons it was chosen:

  • It is poured straight into the dam and disperses by itself.
  • Use rates are low and easy to measure.
  • It works at high water pH and where algae growth is heaviest.
  • Gilba describes it as non-toxic to fish, and the treated water can still be used to irrigate turf.

Where else it can be used

Agri-Tec is listed for algae including Chara and Phormidium, and for use in farm dams, ponds, irrigation channels, lakes and potable water supplies, so the same approach can apply well beyond golf courses.

What happened

The bloom was visibly affected within three days. Within a week, control was complete and there was no sign of blue-green algae left in the dam.

Keeping it from coming back

Treating the bloom is only half the job. Cutting the nutrients that feed algae matters too, and Gilba suggests slow-release fertilisers on surrounding turf to limit what washes into the water. Gilba's guide to aquatic weed and algae control for dams covers prevention in more detail.
